Candidate A
Lambda Trace

EdTech & CareerTech Suite
Lockup — Nav size
Favicon — 24/48/64
Design intent: Greek lambda (Λ) reading as a mountain peak / apex, with circuit-trace dots on the diagonals suggesting deterministic logic. The dashed horizontal cross-trace at the midpoint hints at the "decision graph" framing in CONTRACT §3.1's downstream_handoff_candidates.
  • Pros: Reads as both apex (peak) and lambda (computation). Scales cleanly to favicon (drop interior dots, keep silhouette). The dashed cross-trace is the most distinctive element — visually unique vs. generic peak logos.
  • Cons: The interior dots get noisy below 24px and need a separate stripped silhouette variant for very small sizes. Lambda may read as too academic / too STEM for the careertech audience.
  • Best for: If you want to lean into the "deterministic engine" / "graph of tools" framing.

Design intent: Hexagonal frame (suggesting modular tooling, network nodes) with an "AL" monogram inside. The A is a triangular wedge echoing the apex/peak idea; the L's terminal dot is a deliberate echo of the AINumbers gold-dot motif (subtle sister-suite kinship without copying).
  • Pros: Most readable as a brand mark — "AL" is unambiguously the suite name. Hex frame works well as a sticker/social avatar. Inverted favicon (filled hex) is high-contrast at small sizes.
  • Cons: Hexagons read as "blockchain / web3" to some audiences — may want to verify with the consulting client base. Two-letter monograms can feel agency-generic.
  • Best for: If brand recognizability and badge-style usage matter more than conceptual storytelling.
Candidate C
Compass Wedge
Mark — 120 px
Apex Logics.org
EdTech & CareerTech Suite
Lockup — Nav size
Favicon — 24/48/64
Design intent: A compass with a north-pointing wedge — the wedge is both an arrow (career upward) and an apex. The compass framing speaks directly to career navigation, the suite's most resonant journey. The wedge's notched bottom (V-cut) gives it some computational angularity instead of reading as a generic arrow.
  • Pros: Strongest narrative fit — "navigate your career with deterministic tools" is the elevator pitch and this mark reads it back. Favicon strips beautifully to just the wedge (high contrast). The compass framing is also legible at small sizes if you keep the ring.
  • Cons: Compass marks are common (think: MapQuest, Foursquare, every consulting firm). The wedge needs to feel sharp not generic — current rendering may need a slight asymmetry or stylization to feel proprietary.
  • Best for: If you want the mark to do narrative work and reinforce the "navigation" framing without explanation.
