Recruiting-Channel Reality
MiM → analyst-level entry. MiM graduates enter the same pipelines as top undergraduates — analyst/junior consultant roles at MBB, Big 4, banks, and corporates. The degree buys access and a brand reset, not seniority. In Europe, MBB and bulge-bracket banks recruit MiM cohorts directly; in the US, MiM grads mostly compete in undergraduate-adjacent recruiting.[1][3]

MBA → associate/manager-level entry. Full-time MBA recruiting feeds associate-level consulting and finance roles and manager-track LDPs — typically 2–3 rungs above MiM entry, reflecting the 3–5 years of pre-MBA experience. This is why the MBA uplift is larger in absolute dollars but arrives later and on a much larger opportunity-cost base.[2]

You cannot trade one for the other. A MiM does not grant associate-level entry, and waiting for an MBA forfeits the early compounding years. That timing asymmetry — small uplift early vs. large uplift late — is exactly what Tab 1 prices.
International Candidates — STEM & Visa Notes
US: Many US MiM/MSM programs carry STEM designation, qualifying international graduates for the 24-month STEM OPT extension (36 months total work authorization vs. 12 for non-STEM).[4] Check the program's CIP code before applying — it changes the H-1B lottery math materially (≈3 lottery attempts vs. 1). Most top US MBA programs now also offer STEM tracks. Model your post-graduation runway in AL-59 STEM OPT Runway Planner.

Europe: Post-study work visas are generally more accessible (e.g., 2-year UK Graduate Route, French APS, German job-seeker visa), which is part of why the European MiM market is deep — international students can actually convert the degree into local employment.

Asia: Work-visa conversion varies widely; Singapore and Hong Kong are the most tractable for international MiM graduates.
