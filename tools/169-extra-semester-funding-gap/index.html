Student Finance & Debt · #169

Extra-Semester
Funding Gap Planner

Under 50% of students finish in four years and only about 63% finish in six. A fifth year or extra semester hits four federal and institutional cliffs at once: the Pell 600% lifetime limit, federal loan aggregate caps, a scholarship semester cutoff, and the SAP 150% maximum timeframe rule. This models which cliff binds first, semester by semester, and ranks the cheapest order to fill whatever gap remains.

Data: 2025–26 federal aggregate loan limits (dependent $31,000 / independent $57,500 undergraduate); Pell Lifetime Eligibility Used cap 600% (statutory, HEA); SAP 150% maximum timeframe (federal minimum standard). All figures overridable. Check your school's financial aid office and your own award letters for exact numbers.

▲ The Four Cliffs Pell grants stop once you have used 600% of your Lifetime Eligibility Used (LEU): full-time enrollment typically consumes 50% per semester, so roughly six years of full-time Pell use. Federal loan aggregates cap total borrowing at $31,000 (dependent) or $57,500 (independent) for undergraduates. Most institutional and outside scholarships cap the number of semesters they pay for. And Satisfactory Academic Progress (SAP) cuts off all federal aid, Pell and loans together, once attempted credit hours exceed 150% of your program's published length.
