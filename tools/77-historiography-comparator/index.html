Historiography Comparator

Historiography is the history of how historians interpret history. The same event — the French Revolution, the fall of Rome, the Industrial Revolution — looks radically different depending on whether you're asking about great leaders, economic structures, social forces, or cultural meanings. This tool maps five major interpretive schools onto ten canonical historical events so you can see how the questions a school asks shape the answers it finds.

Data vintage: Historiographical schools: Burke (2001), New Perspectives on Historical Writing; Evans (1997), In Defence of History; Stanford (1998), A Companion to the Study of History

The Five Schools at a Glance
Traditional / Great Man
Leaders, statesmen, commanders. Narrative driven by exceptional individuals. Chronological storytelling.
Annales / Social
Long-term structures: geography, climate, demography, economy. Skeptical of event-driven history.
Marxist / Economic
Class struggle, modes of production, economic determinism. History driven by material contradictions.
Revisionist
Questions prevailing orthodoxies; rehabilitates "losers"; challenges national myths. Varies by event.
Postmodern / Cultural
Representations, discourse, memory, identity. Questions grand narratives. Marginalized voices.
