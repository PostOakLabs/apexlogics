Anachronism Detector

An anachronism is something out of its time — a telephone in ancient Rome, the phrase "OK" in 1840s England, a corporation in medieval Europe. Spot the Anachronism Quiz 5 Excerpts

Each excerpt is set in a specific historical period and contains one or more deliberate anachronisms. Read each passage, enter your guess, then reveal the answer.

Excerpt 1 — Set in 1492 (Columbus's voyage)
"Columbus gazed at the compass and consulted his telescope to scan the horizon. His crew was getting restless, and he noted their anxieties in his journal."
Anachronism: The telescope. Columbus used a compass (which was available — Chinese magnetic compass c.1040, European use c.1190), but the telescope was not invented until 1608 (Hans Lipperhey). Columbus had no optical telescope. His navigation used dead reckoning and a compass, not telescopic observation.
Excerpt 2 — Set in medieval Europe, 13th century
"The guildhall of the corporation met to discuss their insurance policies and decide how to allocate stock dividends among the partners."
Anachronisms: Corporation (East India Company 1600; modern corporate form 19th century), insurance policies (marine insurance contracts first appear in Genoa, 1347 — so this is barely possible by late 13th century but the formal concept of insurance as practiced was later), and stock dividends (Amsterdam Stock Exchange 1602). The guildhall itself is period-appropriate, but the corporate and financial mechanisms are not.
Excerpt 3 — Set in ancient Rome, 50 CE
"The centurion spoke into his radio to coordinate the ambush, while another soldier used his revolver to hold the prisoner at bay."
Anachronisms: Radio (Marconi's first radio transmission 1895 — 1,845 years after this scene) and revolver (Colt's revolver patent 1836 — 1,786 years after this scene). Roman legionaries used gladii, pila, and shields. Both are extreme anachronisms.
Excerpt 4 — Set in 1776 America
"Benjamin Franklin published the pamphlet in the newspaper, then boarded the railroad to Philadelphia to present it to the Continental Congress."
Anachronism: The railroad. Franklin could plausibly publish in a newspaper (the first American newspaper dates to 1690; the Pennsylvania Gazette was published from 1728). But the steam locomotive was not invented until 1804, and commercial rail service began in 1825. In 1776, Franklin would have traveled by horse, coach, or boat. The railroad is 28–49 years too early.
Excerpt 5 — Set in Renaissance Florence, 1490
"Leonardo read the newspaper while across the workshop, the scientist peered through his microscope at a specimen of Florentine moss."
Anachronisms: Newspaper (first printed newspaper: Relation, 1605, Strasbourg — 115 years too early), and the word "scientist" (coined by William Whewell in 1833 — 343 years too early; in 1490, one would say "natural philosopher"). The microscope is marginal: attributed to Zacharias Janssen c.1590 — possible within a century of this scene, but not yet in 1490. Leonardo himself designed instruments but had no compound microscope.
